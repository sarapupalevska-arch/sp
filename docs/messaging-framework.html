Why a new framework

In the positioning doc, I set out a value-focused framework: the value we bring to clients, the worry each one answers and what we say about it. It gave us the right ingredients, but not a clear hierarchy.

It was never approved, so we didn't use it as the basis for the homepage copy. This is a different approach. It connects our positioning directly to our messaging, and makes us get clear and aligned on our main promise, our pillars and the proof behind them before we write any copy.

Messaging pyramid

The structure: what we need to define, from the main promise down to the proof

Where it comes from

A standard model in brand and content strategy, also called a messaging hierarchy. It has no single author: brand strategists and communications teams developed it to rank a company's messages by importance, with one core promise at the top and proof at the base. Erin Kissane describes it in The Elements of Content Strategy as the scaffolding for a company's content.

Context

First principles thinking

What goes in each box, built only from what is true

Where it comes from

The idea goes back to Aristotle, who described a first principle as the most basic truth something can be known from. It is widely used today in strategy and engineering: break a problem down to what you know is true, then build up from there instead of copying what others do. For us, that means messages built from facts about our ICP and how we work, not from what other recruiters say.

  • Final wording

A separate decision

Important question to get clear on before we populate our messaging pyramid

How are we selling JobRack?

Humans only spend money on three things: solutions, convenience, and experiences.

A popular sales maxim, drawing on Clayton Christensen's Jobs-to-be-Done theory and B. Joseph Pine II and James H. Gilmore's The Experience Economy (1998).

What we deliverA service
Why someone pays for itA solution, convenience or an experience?

A service can be sold as any of the three. We need to agree which one we lead with.

1

A solution

that removes pain

We sellthe removal of the problem: the founder has become the hiring department, and we take that off their desk.

StrengthThe most specific to our ICP.

RiskIt can sound abstract if the pain isn't vivid.

2

Convenience

that saves time

We sellspeed and ease: a shortlist by week 3 and a hire in 3 to 4 weeks.

StrengthThe most concrete, and we can prove it.

RiskSpeed and ease are what every recruiter and job board claims, so it invites comparison on price and speed.

3

An experience

that changes how someone feels

We sellconfidence: hiring someone overseas without the fear of getting it wrong.

StrengthIt speaks to the anxiety behind the decision.

RiskConfidence is easy to claim and hard to believe without strong proof.

JobRack is a combination of all three. But our messaging needs one lead, and that is the decision I'd like us to make together.

Whichever we choose, the other two still appear in our messaging as proof and reassurance.
