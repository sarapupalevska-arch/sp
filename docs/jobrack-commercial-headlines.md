# JobRack commercial headlines: main message + pillars 1–9

Produced by seven specialist copywriters (website, sales, CRO, Noel-voice study, voice-of-customer, positioning strategist, headline wordsmith), merged, then put through a hostile review read first as Noel and then as a cold agency founder. Framework source: Messaging-framework (2).html, "edited after Noel's feedback" column used as fact.

## The set

| # | Headline | Job it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Main** | Stop being your agency's hiring department. We run the hire and tell you who we'd pick. | Pain, then both halves of the edited promise: the whole hire is ours, and you get a recommendation. |
| **1** | Experienced people, 30–50% less than a comparable US or UK salary. | What the person costs. "Experienced" so it cannot read as cheap. |
| **2** | One founder's perfect hire is another's nightmare. We find yours. | Chosen for you (selection only). Nothing about English, hours or contract. |
| **3** | On your contract, in your meetings, one of your team. | After they are chosen, they function as your team (integration only). |
| **4** | Four weeks, with every candidate tested first. | Speed without giving up judgment. |
| **5** | $6,500 flat. We earn the same whoever you hire. | Our incentive never tilts your decision. Not "cheap". |
| **6** | Not thrilled with anyone? Refunded. They leave within a year? Replaced free. | Our money is at risk, in two specific commitments. |
| **7** | You don't write the job ad, sift applicants or run the screening calls. We do. | The work we carry, named task by task. |
| **8** | We watch them do your kind of work before we put their name in front of you. | Human judgment on real work. The recommendation itself sits in the Main. |
| **9** | A partner who knows your business well enough to disagree with you. | Closeness and candour: understanding earns the right to be straight. |

Main ladder: promise → person (1–3) → process (4–6) → partnership (7–9). The three groups belong in the Main's subline, because the edited promise never mentions the person.

## Best alternates (from the specialist files)

- **Main:** "Tell us the role. We'll tell you who to hire." (wordsmith, tightest) · "You run the agency. We run the hire, and tell you who we'd hire." (website) · "Hire a great team member without becoming the hiring department." (CRO)
- **1:** "Senior people. 30–50% less than a comparable local hire." (wordsmith) · "The senior hire you'd have put off at local rates." (website)
- **2:** "Hand-picked for the role you actually have." (wordsmith)
- **3:** "Your team member, on your contract." (positioning)
- **4:** "Quick to hire. Slow to wave anyone through." (wordsmith)
- **5:** "One flat $6,500. It never changes with who you hire." (website)
- **6:** "Not thrilled with anyone we shortlist? You get your money back." (website/CRO)
- **7:** "Give us the brief. We do the rest, until you decide." (website) — dropped from the top slot: collides with Main and conflicts with the framework, where we write the job description.
- **8:** "We watch them do your work, then say who we'd hire." (wordsmith) — use only if the Main's tail is rephrased.
- **9:** "One senior partner who knows your business, and tells you straight." (wordsmith, fallback)

## Rules the set was held to

- One distinct first principle per pillar; 2 vs 3, 5 vs 6 and 7 vs 8 vs 9 were checked specifically.
- "Hiring department" appears only in the Main. Main is unavoidably P7 plus the recommendation; P7 elaborates rather than repeats.
- No banned words, no unverified numbers. The only questions are in P6, each the guarantee's own condition.
- No "$6,500 vs 15–25%" line: $6,500 is not cheaper than a 15–25% fee below roughly $26k–$43k salary, which covers many Eastern Europe and South Africa roles.

## Questions for Noel before anything is final

1. **P1:** does "30–50% less" mean salary, or total hiring cost? The framework proof line says "lower hiring cost"; the pillar says "less expensive talent".
2. **P6:** is the refund of the upfront fee or the whole fee? The Playbook says upfront; the framework says "a refund".
3. **P9:** is he comfortable promising a partner who will disagree? Fallback above.
4. **P4:** "four weeks" is an average, not a guarantee. Keep the bare number in the headline, or say "about"?
5. **P2:** the specialist reported this line as adapted from Noel's own emails ("One person's perfect hire is another person's nightmare"). Confirm it is his before crediting him.
6. **Main:** "hiring department" is framework language, not proven ICP wording (no sourced founder said it). Treat as a bet.

## Framework issues found

- The P2 "edited" description cell is a copy of P3's text, so P2 has no description of its own.
- "Hold the client's hand every step" was added to P8 but belongs in P9. "Support through the hiring decision" sits in P7 but is P8's recommendation.
- Role/business understanding is claimed by both P8 and P9. Suggested split: P8 the role and the person, P9 the business and its owner.
- Salary/market guidance appears in both P1 and P9.
- "Equal-footing partnership" honestly fits only P9.
- Gap: nothing says "we understand agencies", though Noel's Playbook does.

## Limits of this work

- jobrack.eu was blocked, so the current site copy was not seen and nothing was checked for repeats.
- No Slack or call transcripts were reachable. Noel's voice comes from 14 newsletter emails and the Remote Hiring Playbook.
- The ICP language bank rests on Drive testimonials and job briefs. No first-hand Reddit or review-site quotes could be retrieved. Real call or Loom replies would tighten it.
- Unverified figures kept out of headlines: 1,000+ hires (Noel's emails also say "more than 1,000 clients"), 90%+ retention, 1 in 3 rehire, "15–25% elsewhere", the Playbook's 40+ hours per DIY hire.
